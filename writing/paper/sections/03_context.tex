\section{Course Context}
\label{sec:context}
% Budget: ~0.75 page. Anonymise: no university name, no course code.

The course is a third-year undergraduate capstone at a large research university. Over a
12-week semester, teams of five or six students build software for a real client. The
course has no formal sprints; the semester is organised around assessment milestones.
Early on, teams pitch a project and write a proposal, and in weeks 10 and 11 they
user-test their software. The largest piece of assessment is the delivered
project. A group report, an individual reflection, a final presentation and a poster for a
public showcase make up most of the rest.

Each team has a tutor who meets it every week and marks its members' journals. In the
semester reported here, 43 teams (26 of six students and 17 of five) were split among six
tutors, seven or eight teams each.

Every student submits five journals over the semester, roughly one a fortnight. Each
follows a fixed template. For every week it covers, the student records what they did,
the most significant events and how they felt about them, what they learned, and what they
plan to do next. A separate section asks them to ``analyse the behavioural relationships
between the members of your team and its impact on team's performance''. A timesheet of
hours worked closes the journal.

We used the third journal of the semester, which covers weeks 6 and 7
(Section~\ref{sec:practice}). It came to 222 submissions with a median length of about
1,500 words, about 370,000 words in total. Spread across six tutors, that is roughly 37
journals and 60,000 words each for a single round.

Students also rate each other's contribution four times a semester, and the ratings adjust
individual marks. From 2026 the course weights them with a normalisation similar to
WebPA~\cite{loddington2009webpa} that leaves out self-ratings, to counter collusion
around self-scores. The ratings say how much each member contributed but not what
happened between members, which is what the journals' team section asks about.
