# Per-type deep dive — open exploration beyond the checklist

**Date:** 2026-09-01. Companion to llm-results.md and llm-type-portraits.md.

Reading *within* each cascade type for recurring themes the 13-item checklist does
not capture. **Descriptive and exploratory** — cells are small (n=8) and the source
is the 72B's per-team notes (not raw journals), so these are grounded **leads**, not
tallies. Names scrubbed to roles/labels.

---

## Contested — the "contest" is deliberative, not dysfunctional

Contested reads *calm* on the interpersonal checklist (harmonious 75%, low
under-contribution). The open read explains why: **the checklist measures
interpersonal dysfunction — slacking, singling-out, festering conflict — and
Contested teams mostly lack those. Their characteristic friction is a different
kind.**

**Theme 1 — friction is structural (front-end/back-end integration), and it gets
aired and resolved (~4/8).** The tension is about *merging work and making
decisions*, not about people:
- a "voting system" for controversial features; back-end progress criticised for
  quality (team_10);
- "tension during code integration, inconsistencies between different parts" (team_02);
- "format mismatches between frontend and backend… responded maturely, re-established
  communication protocols" (2025_s1 team_17);
- a conflict over *perceived* effort "addressed in an open discussion and resolved"
  (2023_s2 team_17).
This is very plausibly *why the peer ratings split* — members genuinely debate
direction/quality/effort — without the team being broken. Deliberation, not decay.

**Theme 2 — a factional minority (~1–2/8).** The exception is a genuinely divided
team: a clear under-contributor (sarcastic, criticising without contributing),
the designated leader bypassed, and a **language-based subgroup that met separately
and excluded others** (2023_s2 team_18). This is the darker face of "contested" — a
clique split rather than a debate.

**Theme 3 — language/culture as a cohesion factor (~3/8; checklist-invisible).**
It cuts both ways: shared background "facilitating smooth communication" (team_24)
vs a language subgroup fracturing the team (team_18) vs "language barriers… better
after a task board" (team_10).

**Reading:** Contested is not one thing. Mostly it is **deliberative teams whose
friction is about the work and gets resolved**; a minority are **factional**, often
on a culture/language seam. Both fit "raters disagree about the team" far better than
the interpersonal checklist did — and both are patterns the checklist never held.

## Both-ends vs One-at-top — same imbalance, opposite handling

Both types have the *same* high divergence (~4.5–4.6) and the same core structure
(a carrying core + weak contributor). What differs is **how the imbalance is
handled** — and it maps exactly onto the checklist gap (Both-ends open-conflict 0%,
One-at-top elevated conflict + communication breakdown).

**One-at-top — the imbalance is VOICED.** The over-carrier vents, frustration
surfaces, leadership gets contested, communication strains:
- carriers "explicitly dissatisfied with contribution percentages," a leader
  "stressed and overworked" (2023_s2 team_06);
- carriers "reached a breaking point and decided to discuss it with the lecturers"
  (2023_s2 team_02);
- conflicts "addressed but not fully resolved… root causes not addressed" amid a
  leadership vacuum (2025_s1 team_08).
The story is told from the over-functioning member's frustrated point of view. It's
loud.

**Both-ends — the imbalance is WORKED AROUND, silently.** A clear under-contributor
is named, but the team routes around them rather than confronting:
- "the team did not address this directly, preferring to work around it" (2025_s1 team_19);
- "avoidance of direct confrontation… worked around her absence" (2024_s2 team_19);
- "conflict was largely unaddressed… no evidence the team formally addressed the
  underperformance" (2023_s2 team_12).
Low overt conflict not because things are fine, but because confrontation is
*avoided*. A freeze-out, not a fight.

**New theme — a *perception disconnect* (Both-ends especially; checklist-invisible).**
The under-contributors often don't perceive the problem the carriers do: *"C and E
maintained a positive outlook despite being identified as under-contributors —
a disconnect in perceptions"* (team_12). The "both ends" are ends of **perception**,
not just contribution — which is a compelling reason the *peer ratings* split into
high and low camps.

**New theme — assessment as a covert conflict channel.** Teams that avoid
confrontation register displeasure *through the mechanism* instead — hoping it's
"reflected in grading" (team_12) or escalating to lecturers/peer-review rather than
raising it with the member. The friction is real; it just travels through the
assessment rather than the conversation.

(Both types include milder teams too — One-at-top 09/14/05 and Both-ends 37 are
fairly balanced with a single accommodated weak member — so the tendency is a
centre of gravity, not a rule. n=8 each; leads, not tallies.)
