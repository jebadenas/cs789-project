## 3. Attack results — RQ1 (3 min)

**RQ1 asks:** Can students manipulate their IWF score, and by how much?

We ran **four attack strategies** on the 217 real clean matrices.
"Mean delta" = average IWF change caused by the attack (higher = more damage).

| Attack | What it is | Most vulnerable model | Mean Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Targeted downvote | One student gives another a zero | WebPA | **2.78** |
| Uniform inflation | A group all give each other top scores | Baseline | **2.94** |
| Zero-self (full ring) | A colluding group all give each other 0 self-score | Baseline | **2.11** |
| Single outlier | One extreme score from one rater | WebPA | **0.78** |

Key findings:
- **Targeted downvote is the most powerful single-actor attack** — a ~2.8 point swing.
- **Iterative models (PeerRank, PeerHITS) have a natural defence against collusion rings** — their zero-self delta is 0.0 because credibility weighting neutralises the ring.
- **Baseline is most vulnerable to collusion** (no weighting mechanism).
- **Profile sweep** (running the same attacks across different simulated rater behaviours) confirmed results are stable regardless of the assumed rating style.

## 4. RQ2 — convergence (1 min)

**RQ2 asks:** Do the iterative models converge reliably?

Short answer: **yes, always, on this dataset**.

- PeerRank (both variants): converges in ~100 iterations at α=0.1, no failures.
- PeerHITS: converges in ~10 iterations, much faster than PeerRank.
- At the strictest threshold (α=0.05), PeerRank takes up to 710 iterations max — but still converges.
- 4–5 matrices cause errors (these are the same degenerate cases we already exclude).

No convergence concerns to raise with her — but worth confirming the α=0.1 default is acceptable.
